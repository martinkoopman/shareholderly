# BlackRock Investment Stewardship — Proxy Voting Guidelines for U.S. Securities (Effective January 2026)

---

## Director Elections

- **Independence:** BIS looks for a majority of board directors to be independent. All members of the **Audit, Compensation, and Nominating/Governance committees** are expected to be independent as best practice.
- **Non-independent directors:** BIS may vote AGAINST directors deemed non-independent (including at controlled companies). Common impediments to independence include: senior executive employment within the past five years; equity ownership exceeding 20%; or any material conflicting interest or relationship.
- **Attendance:** BIS generally considers attendance below **75%** of full board and applicable committee meetings to be poor. May vote AGAINST a director with a multi-year pattern of poor attendance or poor attendance in a single year with no disclosed rationale.
- **Over-boarding (maximum public board seats):**
  - Public company executives (Named Executive Officers or Executive Chairs): **2 total public boards** (including their own).
  - Non-executive directors: **4 total public boards**.
  - A Chair role at a European listed company may count as **two board commitments**.
- **Risk oversight:** May vote AGAINST audit committee members where the board has not facilitated quality, independent auditing or timely disclosure of material weakness remediation. May vote AGAINST responsible committee members where material risk factor disclosure is inadequate.
- **Classified/staggered boards:** BIS will typically vote AGAINST directors on classified boards when governance concerns cannot be addressed through a timely election; generally supports declassification proposals.
- **Board composition (S&P 500):** May vote AGAINST members of the nominating/governance committee where an S&P 500 company board is a sustained outlier in variety of experiences, perspectives, and skillsets relative to market practice.
- **Nominating/Governance committee:** May vote AGAINST the chair of the nominating/governance committee (or longest-tenured member if no chair) where the board is not sufficiently responsive to shareholders or lacks adequate independence in leadership.
- **Board leadership:** Defers to boards on Chair/CEO structure absent significant governance concerns. Does not typically support shareholder proposals mandating Chair/CEO separation unless there are overarching, sustained governance failures.
- **Poison pills (rights plans) without shareholder approval:** May vote AGAINST the independent Chair or Lead Independent Director and members of the nominating/governance committee.
- **Shareholder responsiveness:** May vote AGAINST relevant directors where a board member received AGAINST votes from **more than 25% of shares voted** at the most recent election and the board has not taken appropriate responsive action.
- **IPO companies:** May apply a one-year grace period for certain director-related guidelines (e.g., overboarding, board composition).
- All director voting decisions are evaluated on a **case-by-case basis** considering company-specific facts.

## Auditor Ratification

- BIS may vote AGAINST audit committee members where the board has not facilitated quality, independent auditing.
- Particular scrutiny is applied in cases of **significant financial restatements** or **material weakness disclosures**; BIS looks for timely disclosure and remediation.
- May vote AGAINST auditor ratification where the auditor has not reasonably identified and addressed issues leading to a significant financial restatement, or the audit firm has violated standards of practice.
- May support shareholder proposals promoting auditor independence or rotation of audit firms consistent with the above views.
- **No explicit non-audit fee ratio or tenure threshold is specified**; evaluation is qualitative and case-by-case.

---

## Say-on-Pay

- BIS evaluates compensation practices at the specific company to inform its vote on Say-on-Pay proposals.
- May vote AGAINST management compensation proposals and/or relevant compensation committee members where **pay and performance are misaligned**.
- BIS generally supports **annual** Say-on-Pay advisory votes. May vote AGAINST compensation committee members if a company does not conduct a Say-on-Pay vote within the most-supported frequency or omits it without explanation.
- Looks for a **clear link between variable pay and operational/financial performance** supporting sustained long-term financial value.
- Evaluates both executive team efforts and outcomes realized by shareholders; payouts should reflect contributions as well as exogenous factors.
- Concerns about excessive executive compensation relative to company performance and/or peers may result in voting AGAINST compensation committee members.
- May vote AGAINST compensation committee members where options are repriced without shareholder approval, or where a repricing includes Named Executive Officers (also may vote AGAINST Say-on-Pay in such cases).
- **No specific CEO-to-median pay ratio threshold is stated**; evaluation is qualitative.

## Equity Plans

- BIS supports equity plans that **align interests** of directors, managers, and employees with shareholders.
- Generally **opposes** plans containing "**evergreen**" provisions (automatic annual share increases without shareholder approval).
- Generally **opposes** plans that allow **repricing without shareholder approval**.
- May **oppose** plans that allow acceleration of vesting on a change of control without actual termination ("single trigger"); encourages **"double trigger"** change-of-control provisions.
- Evaluation of equity plans is based on executive pay and performance relative to peers and whether the plan contributes to a pay-for-performance disconnect.
- May support an equity plan share request if determined to be in shareholders' best interests, but may also vote AGAINST compensation committee members to signal concerns about plan structure or imprudent equity use.
- Typically supports qualified **Employee Stock Purchase Plans (ESPPs)** under Section 423 of the Internal Revenue Code.
- **No explicit burn rate or dilution percentage threshold is stated**; evaluation is qualitative and case-by-case.

## Shareholder Proposals — Environmental & Social

- BIS takes a **case-by-case approach**, focused solely on the proposal's implications for **long-term financial value creation**.
- May support proposals requesting disclosures that help investors better understand **material sustainability-related risks and opportunities** (environmental and social), especially where additive to existing disclosures.
- Supports disclosure of **climate-related risks** consistent with companies' business models and sectors, including transition plans (though transition plan preparation is not a voting issue per se).
- Finds helpful disclosures on: **workforce size, composition, compensation, engagement, turnover, training, working conditions, health, safety, and wellbeing** (human capital management).
- May support proposals relating to **corporate political activities** disclosure where increased transparency helps investors understand governance and oversight processes for managing material risks.
- Does NOT support shareholder proposals viewed as inconsistent with long-term financial value or that seek to **micromanage companies**.
- BIS may convey concerns about board oversight of material climate, nature-related, or stakeholder risks through votes on **director elections** or by supporting **business-relevant shareholder proposals**.

## Shareholder Proposals — Governance

- **Classified boards:** Typically supports proposals requesting **board de-classification**; may make exceptions with appropriate strategic rationale.
- **Cumulative voting:** Generally **opposes** proposals requesting adoption of cumulative voting; prefers majority vote standard.
- **Majority voting:** Generally **supports** proposals seeking to introduce majority vote standard for director elections; supports resignation policies for directors who fail to receive majority support.
- **Supermajority provisions:** Generally **supports** elimination of supermajority voting requirements; may support supermajority in the presence of a dominant/substantial shareholder to protect minority interests.
- **Proxy access:** Supports **market-standardized proxy access** (≥3% ownership for ≥3 years, group of up to 20 shareholders, nominate greater of 2 directors or 20% of board); generally opposes proposals with outlier thresholds where standardized access already exists.
- **Right to call a special meeting:** Supports right to call a special meeting where **15%–25%** of shareholders must agree before a meeting is called; may oppose if structured for benefit of a dominant shareholder.
- **Right to act by written consent:** Considers it good practice when available; requires minimum **50% of outstanding shares** to effectuate action. May oppose if structured for a dominant shareholder's benefit or if a reasonable special meeting right already exists.
- **Independent board chair:** Defers to boards on leadership structure; does NOT typically support shareholder proposals mandating separation of Chair and CEO unless there are overarching, sustained governance failures.
- **Poison pills:** Generally opposes rights plans put to a shareholder vote; may support plans with a reasonable qualifying offer clause and sunset provision, or where necessary to protect tax/economic benefits.
- **Dual/multiple class shares:** Has concerns with differentiated voting rights; generally supports proposals to recapitalize to a single voting class where companies do not voluntarily implement "one share, one vote" within a specified timeframe.
- **Golden parachutes:** Evaluates advisory votes on a case-by-case basis; may support shareholder proposals requiring shareholder approval before golden parachute plans are implemented.
- **Clawback proposals:** Typically supports shareholder proposals unless the company already has a robust clawback policy; favors prompt recoupment for faulty financial reporting or deceptive practices.
- All shareholder proposal votes are assessed on a **case-by-case basis**, with a singular focus on long-term financial value for shareholders.
